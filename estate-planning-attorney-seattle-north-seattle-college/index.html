Wills, Trusts, and Estate Planning Services Offered

Estate planning is one of the firm's practice areas, and it is the area the firm is currently working to grow. That matters for a practical reason. When a firm is building out a practice area, the work is not an occasional favor squeezed between other matters. It is the kind of work the firm is set up to take on and wants to take on.

If you are trying to decide whether to call, the fairest way to think about it is by category of problem rather than by document name. Most people arrive with one of a few concerns: they want their wishes written down, they want someone they trust able to act for them, or they are trying to sort out what happens to a specific asset. Estate planning is the body of law that addresses all three, and it is the area this firm handles.

We would rather you hear the specifics of your situation from an attorney than from a web page. There is a reason for that, and it is not evasiveness. Estate planning is one of the few areas of law where the right answer depends almost entirely on facts that are personal to you: who is in your family, what you own, whether anything is held jointly, whether anyone depends on you, and what you want to happen. Two people the same age with similar incomes can need very different documents.

Washington law also shapes the answer in ways that surprise people. It is a community property state, which affects how married couples hold and pass assets. It has its own statutory framework for probate. It has its own rules for how certain planning documents must be signed and witnessed to be valid. An attorney who practices estate planning in Washington is working inside that framework every day, and the framework is the reason a form downloaded from the internet often does not do what the person filling it out believed it would do.

So the honest description of what happens next is this. You describe your situation. An attorney tells you which estate planning documents fit it, which ones do not apply to you, and what the sequence looks like. Some people leave that conversation needing less than they expected. Some learn they need something they had not considered. Either result is useful, because both replace guessing with a plan.

What this section is not going to do is promise you an outcome or quote you a number. Every matter is different, and Washington's rules on attorney advertising exist precisely because outcomes cannot be guaranteed in advance. What we can tell you plainly is that estate planning is a practice area here, and the firm is investing in it.

If you would rather ask a direct question before booking anything, that is a reasonable way to start. A short call can usually tell you whether your situation is the kind of estate planning matter this firm handles.

Estate planning has a way of sitting on a to-do list for years.
